01

Foundation

What the brand has to do

Just Superb Cars is a family-run yard on Morayfield Road with a 4.8 star Google rating, four decades of trade experience and about 66 cars on the ground at any time. The competition is a strip of identical lots and a national website with a bigger ad budget. The brand's job is to make a small yard feel like the safest and easiest place north of Brisbane to buy, sell or finance a car.

Three things every layout should prove

  • The stock is right here. Cars come first on every page. Search, filter, price, photos. Nothing gets between someone and the vehicle list.
  • The people are real. Named humans, actual customer handover photos, the licence numbers in plain sight. Stock photography reads as a scam in this category.
  • Three doors, one yard. Buy a car, sell a car, get finance. Those are the three journeys. Every page makes it obvious which door you're standing at.

Personality

We are

Straight-talking, local, quick to answer, confident about price. Bold typography and hard colour, because a car yard is not a boutique.

We are not

Corporate, apologetic, or padded with phrases like "automotive excellence" and "unparalleled difference". If a sentence could belong to any dealer in Australia, cut it.

02

Logo

The wordmark stays exactly as it is

The lockup is JUST SUPERB CARS set in Impact, tightly kerned, with the three words carrying the three brand colours. It is already recognised on the signage, the flags and the windscreen stickers, so the redesign leaves it untouched. Impact is used for the logo only. It never appears as a heading, a button, or body copy anywhere else.

Primary  /  light backgrounds
Reversed  /  ink and photography
Never Purple on purple. SUPERB vanishes.

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space on all four sides equals the cap height of the letter J. Nothing enters that zone: no text, no button, no photo edge, no other logo. Minimum reproduction width is 140px on screen and 32mm in print. Below that the three colour breaks stop reading and you should use a single-colour version instead.

Rules

Do

  • Reverse JUST to white when the background is darker than Ink 500.
  • Place it on flat colour, or on the darkened area of a photo where contrast is at least 4.5:1.
  • Keep the horizontal lockup as the default. The angled version from the current footer is a decorative variant for merchandise only.

Don't

  • Re-colour, re-space, outline, emboss or add a drop shadow.
  • Stretch, condense or rotate the horizontal lockup.
  • Set it over a busy part of a photo, or on Purple 600 where SUPERB disappears.
  • Rebuild it in Roc Grotesk. The mark is Impact and stays Impact.

Typography

Roc Grotesk, everywhere except the logo

One typeface for the whole brand. Roc Grotesk by Nikola Kostić (Kostić Type Foundry) is a neo-grotesque built in nine weights across five widths, which is exactly what a dealership needs: an extra-wide heavy for price and headline moments, a clean regular for spec tables and legal lines, and everything in between without switching families.

Rules

Do

  • Tighten tracking as size goes up. Big type needs negative letter-spacing, small caps labels need positive.
  • Use the Wide widths for price and headline only. That width is the brand's signature.
  • Set body copy at 17px with a 66 character measure and 1.6 line height.
  • Use tabular figures in price lists and spec tables so the columns line up.

Don't

  • Use italics. The current site's slanted headings are a browser-faked oblique and they look it. Roc Grotesk has no true italic.
  • Set paragraphs centred. Centre a headline if you must, never a block of copy.
  • Go below 14px for anything a customer needs to read, including disclaimers.
  • Mix in a second family. If something needs to feel different, change the width or weight.

Green flags mark opportunity, purple marks a service, outlined pills are filters, and the red outline is the only place stock status appears. Never more than one green flag on a card.

Form field

We'll call back within the hour, business hours.

A Queensland rego is 3 letters and 3 numbers, like 902 XOC.

Labels sit above the field, always visible, never a placeholder standing in for a label. Help text explains the format before the person gets it wrong. Error messages say what to do, not what went wrong. The trade-in and finance forms are the two highest-value things on the site, so they get the most generous spacing on the page.

The active item is marked with a 2px green underline, not a colour change, so it reads at a glance. The phone number is a button, not a link. On mobile it collapses to a sticky bar at the bottom of the screen with two actions: Call and Get a valuation.

07

Photography

Real cars, real customers, real yard

The handover photos already on the site are the single most valuable brand asset the business has and the redesign should use far more of them, not fewer. They do something no stock library can: they prove people actually buy here and drive away happy.

Do

  • Stock shots: three-quarter front, shot at the yard, car filling the frame, taken between 8am and 10am or after 4pm to avoid blown highlights.
  • Handover shots: customer and car together, natural expression, the green flag or signage somewhere in frame.
  • Shoot landscape 4:3 for cards and 16:9 for banners. Same crop across every listing.
  • Keep the yellow windscreen price stickers visible in atmosphere shots. That's the yard's real texture.

Don't

  • Use stock photography of cars, keys, handshakes or money. Every one of those reads as a template.
  • Desaturate hero images to grey the way the current homepage does. Darken with an Ink overlay instead so the colour survives.
  • Crop a customer's face or plate out awkwardly. Get permission, or don't use the photo.
  • Mix portrait and landscape crops in the same grid.

Overlays

When type sits on a photo, use a linear Ink overlay from rgba(15,15,22,.72) at the type edge to rgba(15,15,22,.25) away from it. Never a flat grey filter across the whole image. Measure the contrast on the darkest text corner and hold 4.5:1.

Words on screen

How the interface talks

Not a full tone-of-voice document, just the rules that keep the interface consistent while the site is rebuilt. The current copy repeats the same paragraph on four pages and describes the business as "the perfect fusion of top-notch service and exceptional vehicles". None of that survives.

Instead ofWriteWhy
Experience automotive excellence66 cars on the ground, all roadworthiedA number beats an adjective
SubmitGet my valuationSay what the button does for them
Sell my car for cash!Sell your carSecond person, no exclamation marks
Our reveiws speak for themselves4.8 stars from 300+ Google reviewsSpell-check, then use the actual figure
Please note we only purchase…We buy registered, running cars onlyLead with the rule, drop the apology
Simply Application ProcessApply in about five minutesFix the grammar, add the specific
  • Sentence case for buttons, headings and labels. ALL CAPS only for the small mono labels in the type scale.
  • Australian spelling. Tyre, kilometres, licence, colour. The current site says "specialize" and "inquire".
  • Prices always carry their qualifier. "$20,990 excl. govt. charges" or "drive away". Pick one convention and use it site-wide.
  • Finance copy stays factual. No rate promises, no "guaranteed approval". Credit One is the broker and gets named as such.
  • No em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma.

Before this goes live, check

  • Every button label is a verb and describes its own outcome.
  • No text sits on Green 400 except Ink 900.
  • The logo has its clear space on every page, including the mobile header.
  • Body copy holds a 66 character measure at desktop width.
  • All spacing values are on the 4px scale.
  • The page works in both light and dark, and at 320px wide.
  • Prices carry a qualifier and the finance page names Credit One as the broker.
