The "False Positive" Myth

"But I heard a story about a positive result..."

While Police say the tests are specific to Methamphetamine, experts and drivers report a "Reality Gap." Here is why those stories exist:

1

Cross-Reactivity

Some immunoassay tests used at the roadside aren't perfect. Molecular similarities between Dexamphetamine and Methamphetamine can occasionally trigger a "presumptive positive" on a cheap or faulty screening kit.

2

The "Stand Down" Period

If you fail a roadside test, you are often banned from driving for 24 hours before the lab results come back. This is where most horror stories come from—innocent people losing their car for a day.

3

Dosage Levels

Very high therapeutic doses or recent ingestion (taking your pill 10 mins before a stop) can lead to higher saliva concentrations, increasing the risk of a kit error.

What to do if it happens:

  • Remain Calm: A roadside "positive" is NOT a conviction. It is a "presumptive" result that requires lab confirmation.
  • State Your Medication: Inform the officer immediately that you are on prescribed ADHD medication.
  • Wait for the Lab: The lab uses Mass Spectrometry (GC/MS) which cannot be fooled. It sees the exact difference between Dex and Meth.

"99% of 'false positives' for ADHD medication are overturned once the sample reaches a government forensic laboratory."

Chemical Fingerprints

Why the two drugs look different to a scientist.

Dexamphetamine

A simple amphetamine molecule. Used for ADHD. Most roadside tests are designed to ignore this molecule to avoid clogging the legal system with medical cases.

Medical Grade

Methamphetamine

Contains an extra "Methyl" group. This specific chemical tail is what the roadside kit's antibodies are hunting for. Without this tail, the test should stay negative.

Targeted Illicit

The Lab Never Lies

If you are one of the unlucky few who triggers a roadside kit error, the Government Forensic Lab is your ultimate defense. They use a process called GC/MS (Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry).

Molecular Weight

The lab weighs every molecule. Dex and Meth have different weights.

Purity Profile

Prescription meds are 100% pure; street drugs contain tell-tale impurities.

Legal Immunity

If only therapeutic levels of your prescribed med are found, no charge is laid.

Expert FAQ

Saliva concentrations are highest immediately after swallowing a pill (if it touches the mouth) or if using sublingual methods. While it shouldn't trigger the "Meth" line, it increases the chance of a kit error. Drinking water after taking your meds is a good habit.
Legally, they don't have to "believe" you at the roadside. Their job is to follow the kit result. Your job is to ensure your medication is noted on the police report so the lab knows what they are looking for when the sample arrives.
Vyvanse (Lisdexamfetamine) is a "prodrug." It is inactive until it hits your blood. Because of this, it is even less likely to show up in a saliva test than instant-release Dexamphetamine.
